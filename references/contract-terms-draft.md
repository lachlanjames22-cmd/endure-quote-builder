ENDURE DECKS — CONTRACT TERMS & CONDITIONS
DRAFT FOR LEGAL REVIEW — NOT FOR CLIENT USE
Prepared as a working draft for review by a WA construction lawyer. Context: Endure Decks builds residential timber and composite decks in Perth, WA. Written contracts for home building work over $7,500 are regulated by the Home Building Contracts Act 1991 (WA); consumer guarantees under the Australian Consumer Law cannot be excluded. Payment schedule in use: 5% deposit / 35% pre-start / 25% materials delivered / 25% structural complete / 10% handover. The marketing promise is the &quot;Endure 20-Year Standard&quot; — clause 26 turns that into a defined warranty and needs the most careful legal attention.

## 1. The parties and the works
- This contract is between the client named in the accepted quote (&quot;you&quot;) and Endure Decks Pty Ltd [ABN to insert] (&quot;we&quot;, &quot;us&quot;).
- &quot;The works&quot; means the deck and any associated structures described in the accepted quote, built in a proper and workmanlike manner, in accordance with the contract documents.
- We will supply all labour and materials for the works except where the contract documents state otherwise.

## 2. Contract documents and order of precedence
- The contract documents are: (1) any special conditions; (2) these terms; (3) the accepted quote including the price breakdown, inclusions and exclusions; (4) any drawings, sketches or concept plans identified in the quote.
- If the documents conflict, the document earlier in that list prevails.
- This contract replaces all earlier quotes, emails and conversations to the extent they are inconsistent with it.

## 3. Inclusions and exclusions
- Only the items listed in the quote’s inclusions are part of the works and the price. Everything else is excluded — including anything listed in the quote’s &quot;Not included&quot; section.
- Optional upgrades ticked on the approval page are only part of the works once we confirm them in writing with a price, as a variation under clause 10.
- If a quote is based on materially inaccurate information or contains an obvious error, we may decline to proceed and will promptly refund money paid for work not performed.

## 4. Special conditions
- Anything agreed specifically for your job must be written in the &quot;Special conditions&quot; section of the quote — otherwise it does not form part of this contract.

## 5. What you confirm before we start
- You own the property, or you have the owner’s written permission for the works.
- Unless the quote says we are obtaining them, all necessary approvals, permits and licences have been obtained by you, and you have given us copies.
- You have told us everything you know about site conditions that could affect the works (services, easements, soak wells, prior structures, disputes with neighbours affecting access).

## 6. Price and GST
- The contract price is the fixed price in the accepted quote, inclusive of GST unless stated otherwise.
- The price remains valid for acceptance for 30 days from the quote date.
- If commencement is delayed more than 90 days by you, we may adjust the price for documented increases in material costs, by written variation.
> ⚖ LAWYER / LACHY TO DECIDE: Confirm the 30-day validity and the 90-day repricing trigger. The example contract’s &quot;contract void and costs taken from monies paid&quot; approach is not recommended.

## 7. Payment
- You pay the contract price by progress payments: 5% deposit on acceptance; 35% at pre-start confirmation; 25% on delivery of major materials to site; 25% at structural completion of the subframe and decking; 10% at practical completion.
- Each progress claim reflects work performed and materials supplied to that stage.
- Progress claims are payable within 7 days of the claim.
> ⚖ LAWYER / LACHY TO DECIDE: HBCA (WA) regulates progress payments for home building work — confirm each stage genuinely tracks value delivered, and confirm the 7-day terms.

## 8. Deposit
- The deposit is 5% of the contract price (below the 6.5% statutory maximum in WA).
- If a required approval is refused despite proper applications, we refund the deposit less reasonable costs actually incurred (documented) up to that point.
- If you end the contract before work starts for reasons other than our breach, we may retain from the deposit our reasonable costs actually incurred and a reasonable amount for time spent, and we refund the balance.
> ⚖ LAWYER / LACHY TO DECIDE: Replace the example’s automatic deposit forfeiture — likely unenforceable as a penalty and inconsistent with HBCA. This &quot;reasonable costs actually incurred&quot; model is the defensible shape.

## 9. Late payment
- If a progress payment is late, we may suspend the works until payment is received, and the contract period extends by the suspension.
- Overdue amounts accrue interest at [rate — e.g. the rate prescribed under the Civil Judgments Enforcement Act 2004 (WA)] from the due date until paid.
- You must reimburse our reasonable costs of recovering overdue amounts.
> ⚖ LAWYER / LACHY TO DECIDE: Set the interest basis. Do NOT adopt the example’s 40% collection-fee clause or the &quot;enter premises with force and repossess&quot; clause — penalty risk, ACL risk, and reputational damage. Removed deliberately.

## 10. Variations
- Either of us may propose a variation. No variation is binding until it is documented in writing (email is fine) with a description and a price, and accepted by both parties.
- Agreed variations are carried out as part of the works, and payment for a variation is due with the next progress claim unless agreed otherwise.
- If a variation is required by an authority, by law, or by unforeseen site conditions under clause 14, and you do not agree to a reasonable variation, either party may end the contract; you pay for work performed and costs incurred to that date.

## 11. Site access and facilities
- You give us and our subcontractors uninterrupted access to the site during working hours for the duration of the works.
- You provide reasonable access to water and electricity at your cost.
- You keep pets and children clear of the work area during works, and secure the site outside working hours where practicable.

## 12. Commencement and completion
- We commence within 28 days of the latest of: contract acceptance; receipt of the pre-start payment; receipt of any required approvals; and the site being ready.
- We bring the works to practical completion within the period stated in the quote, subject to extensions under clause 13.

## 13. Extensions of time
- The contract period extends by the duration of delays we do not cause, including: your instructions or late selections; variations; weather that prevents safe or proper work; supplier or freight delays outside our control; latent site conditions; and force majeure.
- We will tell you in writing of an extension claim within 10 business days of becoming aware of the cause and its likely extent.

## 14. Unforeseen site conditions
- The price assumes normal soil conditions and no concealed obstructions. If we encounter rock, limestone caprock, concrete, buried structures, fill, contamination or other latent conditions, the additional work is a variation under clause 10.
- We will stop, notify you, and price the variation before proceeding with the affected work.

## 15. Underground and concealed services
- You must identify to us all underground and concealed services you are aware of (power, water, gas, reticulation, sewer, NBN) before work starts.
- We use reasonable care, including utility searches where appropriate. Repair or relocation of services that were not identified to us and not reasonably locatable is at your cost.

## 16. Asbestos and hazardous materials
- The works exclude any handling or removal of asbestos or hazardous materials. If suspected material is found, we stop work in the affected area and the removal is arranged by you (or by us as a variation) using a licensed removalist at your cost. The contract period extends accordingly.

## 17. Excavated material
- Unless the quote includes spoil removal, excavated sand, soil and rock is left neatly on site. Surplus new materials remain our property.

## 18. Existing structures, surfaces and levels
- We take reasonable care, but are not responsible for making good paving, lawns, reticulation or garden surfaces disturbed where post footings and access are reasonably required.
- Decks attached to a house are set to the house. If the existing structure is out of level, the deck may follow it; that is not a defect.
- We are not responsible for pre-existing defects in structures we attach to, provided we exercise reasonable care in the connection.

## 19. Timber is a natural product
- Timber varies in colour and grain, and moves with weather: minor checking (surface cracks), cupping within tolerance, raised grain, tannin bleed, fastener staining and colour fade (silvering) are natural characteristics, not defects.
- Board gaps change seasonally with moisture content; gaps within Australian standard tolerances are not defects.
- We select boards to a high standard, but natural feature and variation is part of a hardwood deck’s character.

## 20. Composite and aluminium products
- Composite boards and aluminium subframe systems carry their manufacturer’s warranty, which we pass through to you. The manufacturer’s installation, use and maintenance requirements form part of this contract.
- Manufacturer-published characteristics (e.g. heat retention, minor colour variation between production batches, static build-up) are not defects.

## 21. Exposure and coastal conditions
- In coastal or high-exposure locations, accelerated surface corrosion of fixings and hardware can occur; we use fit-for-purpose fixings for the stated location, and cosmetic surface corrosion is not a structural defect.

## 22. Care and maintenance — a condition of warranty
- We provide a written care guide at handover. Hardwood decks must be cleaned and re-oiled in accordance with the guide (typically within 3 months of handover and then at least every 12 months, or as the guide specifies).
- The clause 26 warranty is conditional on reasonable adherence to the care guide. Keep receipts or records of maintenance.
> ⚖ LAWYER / LACHY TO DECIDE: Align the stated oiling intervals with the actual care guide before signing off.

## 23. Termites and pests
- The works do not include termite management. Damage caused by termites or pests is excluded from our warranties; we recommend including the deck in your regular termite inspection regime.

## 24. Practical completion and handover
- Practical completion is when the works are complete except for minor omissions or defects that do not prevent normal use of the deck.
- We notify you at practical completion and walk the deck with you; agreed minor items are listed and rectified promptly.
- The final (10%) payment is due at practical completion. Possession and normal use of the works passes to you on payment.

## 25. Defects liability period
- For 12 months from practical completion, we will make good, at our cost and within a reasonable time, defects in workmanship or materials notified to us in writing.
- This does not cover damage caused by others, misuse, lack of maintenance, or the natural characteristics described in clauses 19–21.
> ⚖ LAWYER / LACHY TO DECIDE: 12 months is proposed (the example used 12 months; statutory implied warranties in WA run longer regardless — see clause 27).

## 26. The Endure 20-Year Standard (structural warranty)
- We warrant the structural integrity of the deck subframe and its connections (posts, bearers, joists, footings and structural fixings) for 20 years from practical completion: built to plan, to code, and it will not fail structurally in normal residential use.
- If a structural defect covered by this warranty appears, we will rectify it at our cost.
- This warranty covers structure. Decking boards, finishes, oils and appearance are covered by clause 25, clauses 19–21, and any manufacturer warranty — not by the 20-Year Standard.
- This warranty is conditional on: the maintenance in clause 22; no alterations, additional loads (e.g. spas, planters over design load) or attachments without our written approval; and no damage by events outside normal use (fire, flood, impact, earth movement, termites).
- This warranty is transferable to a subsequent owner of the property [on written notice to us].
> ⚖ LAWYER / LACHY TO DECIDE: THE key clause. Confirm scope (structure only), the conditions list, transferability, and how this interacts with insurance and the statutory warranties. This is the contractual form of the brand promise — it must be genuinely honourable for 20 years.

## 27. Your statutory rights
- Nothing in this contract excludes, restricts or modifies any right or guarantee you have under the Australian Consumer Law or the Home Building Contracts Act 1991 (WA), including statutory implied warranties. Our warranties are in addition to those rights.

## 28. Risk, title and insurance
- Materials delivered to site are at your risk for loss or damage not caused by us; title in materials passes to you as they are incorporated into the works or paid for, whichever is first.
- We hold public liability insurance [and workers’ compensation as required by law] and will provide evidence on request.
> ⚖ LAWYER / LACHY TO DECIDE: Confirm the title/risk split and insurance particulars; add contract works insurance if Endure carries it.

## 29. Subcontractors
- We may engage licensed subcontractors for parts of the works; we remain responsible to you for the works. Please direct all instructions to us, not to subcontractors on site.

## 30. Multiple owners
- If more than one person owns the property, each is jointly and severally responsible under this contract, notice to one is notice to all, and any one owner’s signature binds all owners.

## 31. If something goes wrong
- Talk to us first — most issues resolve with a site visit. Either party may give written notice of a dispute; the parties meet (in person or by phone) within 10 business days to attempt resolution.
- If unresolved, either party may refer the matter to Building and Energy (WA) or make a complaint to the State Administrative Tribunal, as applicable. Nothing prevents either party seeking urgent court relief.

## 32. Ending the contract
- Either party may end this contract by written notice if the other party is in substantial breach and has not remedied it within 10 business days of a written notice describing the breach.
- If the contract ends, you pay for work performed and materials ordered or supplied up to the end date; we refund any amounts paid beyond that.

## 33. Entire agreement
- This contract (the documents in clause 2) is the whole agreement. It can only be changed in writing signed by both parties, or by the variation process in clause 10.
- If any clause is found unenforceable, the rest of the contract remains in force.
- This contract is governed by the law of Western Australia.
